An algorithm is a finite sequence of well-defined steps for solving a problem. Good algorithms are correct, terminate, and use resources efficiently.

Big-O notation describes how runtime or memory grows as input size n grows. O(1) is constant time. O(n) grows linearly. O(n^2) grows quadratically and can become expensive quickly for large n.

When comparing algorithms, consider the common case and the worst case. An algorithm that is fast on average but catastrophic in the worst case may be unacceptable for interactive systems.

Sorting is a classic example: insertion sort is simple and fine for small lists, while mergesort and heapsort provide stronger guarantees for larger inputs.
